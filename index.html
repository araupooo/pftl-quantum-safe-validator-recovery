No published PFTL disaster-recovery runbook or key-compromise procedure exists: the PostFiat whitepaper contains no disaster-recovery, key-compromise, amendment, or revocation procedure, and no ML-DSA validator-key or rotation code was found in the postfiatd files checked. 1. Scope and assumptions

In scope: validator signing-key compromise (classical and ML-DSA-65), quorum-member compromise, bridge-witness compromise, validator-set recovery, ledger re-sync after compromise, and amendment-state verification.

  • A1. Validator set n = 35, quorum q = 24 (whitepaper §1.11, §1.9). The testnet reports 53 validators; the 35 figure is the whitepaper's quorum model.
  • A2. Validators sign with ML-DSA-65 from genesis (whitepaper §1.11).
  • A3. Operator acknowledgement 900 s (15 min) — an assumption, not a measurement.
  • A4. Ledger close 4 s (XRPL ledgers close every ~3–5 s; 4 s is the midpoint).
  • A5. Bridge witnesses are a set separate from the 35 validators, signing with domain-separated ML-DSA-65 contexts. Witness set size and rotation rule are not stated in any source read — the bridge runbook is the most speculative part.
  • A6. Amendment verification uses the postfiatd fork rule (67% post-fix); the whitepaper states no amendment rule.

3. Compromise scenarios

3.1 Single validator signing-key compromise (classical)

Key moves to Compromised (SP 800-57 §7); validator isolated; new key generated independently (re-keying, §8.2.3); new manifest published. Quorum unaffected (34 remain). Drill 01: 6/6 checks, simulated recovery 6,348 s. Runbook (a).

3.2 Single validator ML-DSA-65 key compromise

Same shape with ML-DSA-65 sizes; the rotation procedure is PROPOSED — no shipped ML-DSA rotation code exists in postfiatd. Height-924 precedent: legitimate rotation committed, stolen old key rejected. Drill 02: 7/7 checks, simulated recovery 6,388 s. Runbook (a), rotation.md.

3.3 Quorum-member compromise

Boundary is exact: compromised ≥ 12 leaves fewer than 24 honest validators, and the chain halts rather than lowering q. k = 11 is the worst survivable case (24 remain). Recovery rotates members one at a time. Drill 03: 8/8; drill 07 (set recovery, k = 11): 17/17, simulated recovery 39,640 s. Runbooks (b) and (d).

3.4 Bridge-witness compromise

Witness frozen out of attestations, re-keyed under a new domain-separated context, re-admitted only after verification. Witness set size and quorum rule are unstated; this is the least grounded runbook. Drill 04: 6/6, simulated recovery 6,388 s. Runbook (c).

4. Rotation, revocation, rollback protection

Full procedures in rotation.md. Headlines:

  • Classical-to-hybrid ML-DSA rotation (PROPOSED): offline ML-DSA-65 key generation, publish rotation authorization, verify the new key signs, retire the old key.
  • Emergency rotation: pre-generated standby key; drill 05: 6/6, simulated 668 s (~11.1 min).
  • Revocation: manifest sequence 4,294,967,295; drill 06: 8/8, published 1,228 s simulated after detection, within the 24-hour target.
  • Rollback protection: monotonic manifest sequences; compromised keys never re-activated; a rotation is undone only by a further rotation.
  • Quorum-safety gates: remaining honest validators ≥ 24 before, during, and after every step.

6. Amendment-state verification

The postfiatd fork changed the post-fix amendment majority from 80% to 67% (verified in source, commit 0085a1ff); the 2-week hold is unchanged. DERIVED for 35 trusted validators: postfiatd needs 24 votes (35 × 67/100 = 23.45; votes must exceed 23.45), upstream needs 29 (35 × 80/100 = 28.00). The postfiatd AmendmentTable.cpp comment still says “slightly less than 80%” — a stale upstream comment; the code uses 67%. Drill 09 verifies all four boundary cases: 8/8 checks.
